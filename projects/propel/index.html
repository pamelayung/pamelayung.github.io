01

Overview

Propel helps users manage benefits-related information, including EBT balance tracking and support resources. For this redesign, I focused on connecting benefits, grocery inventory, and budgeting so users could make clearer decisions around food and spending.

Role

UX Designer

Focus

Benefits + grocery planning

Output

High-fidelity prototype

02

Problem

People who rely on EBT benefits often need to make careful decisions around grocery spending, food inventory, and upcoming deposits. However, checking a balance alone does not always help users understand what they already have at home, what food may expire soon, or how to plan purchases around their remaining benefits.

How might we help Propel users connect their benefits, grocery inventory, and spending habits so they can make more informed food and budgeting decisions?

03

Research

Our research showed that users need more than a balance number. They need to understand what food they already have, what may expire soon, when their next deposit is coming, and how their spending affects future grocery decisions.

04

Insights

Insight 01

Quick access matters

Users need balance, deposit, and spending information upfront.

Insight 02

Food planning is connected

Grocery inventory and benefits planning should not feel separate.

Insight 03

Reminders reduce waste

Expiration and quantity prompts help users use what they already have.

05

User Flows

06

Prototype

The final prototype focuses on three connected areas: benefits tracking, grocery inventory, and budgeting support.

07

Reflection

This project helped me think more intentionally about how product design can reduce cognitive load. Benefits information can easily become overwhelming, so I focused on designing screens that help users quickly understand what matters and what they can do next.

If I continued this project, I would test the prototype with users to understand whether the dashboard, grocery inventory, and budgeting tools feel clear and useful in real situations.
