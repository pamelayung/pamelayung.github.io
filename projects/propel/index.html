Overview

Propel helps users manage benefits-related information, including EBT balance tracking and support resources. For this redesign, I focused on connecting benefits, grocery inventory, and budgeting so users could make clearer decisions around food and spending.

Role

UX Designer

Focus

Benefits + grocery planning

Output

High-fidelity prototype

02

Problem

People who rely on EBT benefits often need to make careful decisions around grocery spending, food inventory, and upcoming deposits. However, checking a balance alone does not always help users understand what they already have at home, what food may expire soon, or how to plan purchases around their remaining benefits.

How might we help Propel users connect their benefits, grocery inventory, and spending habits so they can make more informed food and budgeting decisions?

03

Research

Our research showed that users need more than a balance number. They need to understand what food they already have, what may expire soon, when their next deposit is coming, and how their spending affects future grocery decisions.

04

Insights

Insight 01

Quick access matters

Users need balance, deposit, and spending information upfront.

Insight 02

Food planning is connected

Grocery inventory and benefits planning should not feel separate.

Insight 03

Reminders reduce waste

Expiration and quantity prompts help users use what they already have.

05

User Flows

06

Prototype

The final prototype focuses on four key screens that connect benefits tracking, spending visibility, receipt review, and grocery inventory management.

07

Testing Results

After creating our initial redesign, we reviewed the screens through a user-testing lens to see whether the experience clearly supported grocery planning, benefit tracking, and spending decisions.

The biggest takeaway was that users needed clearer reminders, more actionable inventory updates, and spending breakdowns that were easier to interpret at a glance.

We focused the redesign around helping EBT users track groceries, pace spending, and reduce the mental effort of remembering what food they already have.

Finding 01

The homepage needed to be more actionable.

The original homepage emphasized offers, but the redesign made grocery reminders more visible so users could act on food expiration dates sooner.

Finding 02

Inventory needed stronger hierarchy.

Users needed to quickly understand how many items they had, what categories they belonged to, and which foods were expiring soon.

Finding 03

Spending details needed context.

The spending breakdown became clearer after adding recent transactions, category colors, and receipt actions.

08

Before & After

Story 01

From generic offers to grocery reminders

The original homepage included EBT balance information and third-party offers, but those offers did not directly support the grocery planning problem we were solving. In the redesign, we replaced that area with grocery reminders so users could immediately see what food is expiring soon and decide whether to keep or remove items from inventory.

Story 02

From a bar chart to scannable inventory cards

The earlier inventory screen used horizontal bar charts, which showed quantities but made the page feel more like a data visualization than a grocery tool. The updated version uses category cards, icons, and urgency labels so users can quickly understand their pantry, identify what expires today, and prioritize what to use next.

Story 03

Making expiration information easier to act on

We refined the inventory screen by adding a search bar, reorganizing categories into cards, and separating items into “expires today” and “expiring soon.” This made the screen easier to scan and helped users understand urgency without needing to interpret a chart.

Story 04

Making spending history easier to understand

The spending breakdown was revised to emphasize remaining balance, recent transactions, category colors, and receipt access. These changes make the screen more useful for users who want to understand where their benefits are going and how much they have left to spend.

09

Reflection

This project helped me think more intentionally about how product design can reduce cognitive load. Benefits information can easily become overwhelming, so I focused on designing screens that help users quickly understand what matters and what they can do next.

If I continued this project, I would test the prototype with users to understand whether the dashboard, grocery inventory, and budgeting tools feel clear and useful in real situations.
